## L2 Review — Mean-Field Projection Proposition (MFP)

I think this is an important clarification. It changes my D2 analysis more than the registration note suggests, although I agree that it does **not itself decide D2**.

### 1. Coherence

**VERDICT: NO CONFLICT.**

I find no conflict with the standing hard core, jeopardy split, E2 scope language, Base-Space Registration v1.1, or Verified Substrate Facts v1.1.

In fact, MFP resolves a tension I had accepted but not fully dissolved in my earlier D2 analysis: the coexistence of **agent-specific encountered conditions** with a macro-valued (Q(\rho,\Psi)). My earlier reading treated those as competing fidelity claims. MFP supplies a coherent level-of-description relationship between them:

stream-level dynamics → (mean-field projection) → MFA dynamics.

On that reading, macro (ρ) appearing in MFA-Q does not entail that any locus or local ground element literally reads population (ρ). It says what the stream-level system becomes after heterogeneity and correlations have been integrated out.

That is compatible with V1 rather than merely analogous to it. V1 already established precisely this interpretive discipline for Λ: aggregate Λ is a theorist-level descriptor and not an agent stimulus.

There is also no conflict with Base-Space Registration v1.1. Claim A concerns the common conditional mapping at stream level. Claim B concerns local configuration-dependent response. Neither requires a macro field to act microscopically. MFP actually makes their level clearer.

The E2 frozen wording also survives untouched because it is explicitly scoped to the implemented additive ungated common signal, not to a claim that every macro quantity appearing in the MFA must be implemented as a broadcast mechanism.

**One precision:** I would avoid saying that *"the underlying theory is stream-level"* without the qualifier supplied by the registered tier. Because MFP is not hard core, that sentence could make the working interpretation sound definitionally settled. Better in future claim language:

> **Under MFP, the MFA is interpreted as the mean-field projection of underlying stream-level dynamics.**

That preserves the tier honestly.

## 2. Tier

**VERDICT: TIER APPROPRIATE.**

I would neither promote it nor demote it.

It should **not yet enter the hard core**, because the existing hard core is stated at the MFA level and does not logically force a unique microscopic realization. An aggregate-feedback mechanism could conceivably produce the same MFA architecture. Calling MFP hard core now would retrospectively add a microfoundation the formal commitments did not themselves establish.

But **exploratory is too weak**. MFP is doing genuine interpretive work across several already-adopted pieces of the program: local-versus-aggregate Λ, the stream ontology, the meaning of MFA parity, and now D2.

"Committed working interpretation" therefore captures the status unusually well:

* it governs how the program currently understands the relation between MFA and substrate;
* it can constrain claims and experimental interpretation;
* it remains revisable;
* and its revision would not itself constitute rejection of the hard core.

I endorse the tier.

## 3. D2 consequence

**VERDICT: RECOMMENDATION CHANGED IN STRENGTH, NOT MERELY FRAMING.**

My previous D2 input said that the **aggregate read had the stronger textual claim** because the manuscript explicitly says Q reads macro ((ρ,\Psi)), while the local read had the stronger substrate-continuity/causal-granularity claim.

Under MFP, I withdraw the inference that textual MFA fidelity gives the **aggregate substrate read** the stronger conceptual claim.

The manuscript still unquestionably gives the stronger textual claim to aggregate (ρ) **at the MFA level**. What no longer follows is:

> MFA-Q reads aggregate (ρ) → an MFA-faithful agent-level realization should therefore make substrate-Q read aggregate (ρ).

MFP blocks that level crossing.

### Revised adversarial balance

**Global-read Q remains valuable**, but I would now characterize it as a **mimicry/control realization**: instantiate the projected MFA equation directly at substrate level and ask what follows. That can be scientifically useful, especially as a comparator.

Its strongest weakness is now clearer: it potentially **reifies the mean field**. The approximation's aggregate variable is installed as a causal broadcast into the microscopic system whose averaging was supposed to generate that aggregate description.

**Local-read Q now has the stronger claim under the registered MFP interpretation.** It preserves stream-level causal locality and puts the substantive burden where a mean-field program normally wants it:

> Do local dynamics, once aggregated, recover the MFA-level relationship?

That is a considerably riskier and more informative test than building the aggregate relationship directly into every local update.

So, before Mike rules D2, my revised input is:

**I now favor local/neighborhood activation as the primary Q activation read, provided that the MFA-parity bridge is made an explicit and sufficiently strong part of the instrument.** Global-read Q remains scientifically coherent as a comparator/mimicry mode, but I no longer regard MFA textual fidelity as an argument for making it the primary substrate mechanism.

The qualification about the parity bridge is load-bearing. Without it, "local is the real mechanism" could simply insulate the substrate from MFA failure. Under MFP, recovery must be earned.

I would therefore require the local-read choice to expose at minimum:

* the local (ρ)-like quantity actually read by each Q update;
* aggregate (ρ(t));
* the population-aggregated Q response;
* departures from the MFA prediction;
* and enough distributional/spatial telemetry to determine whether a failed mean-field recovery arises from heterogeneity/correlation rather than being hidden by the average.

This also changes the interpretation of failure. A local substrate that does **not** recover the MFA behavior under the declared mean-field comparison is evidence against the proposed stream-level realization of the MFA; MFP cannot be invoked afterward as a reason the two were never supposed to agree.

That is the essential price of adopting MFP.

## 4. §4 consequences and §5 fences

**VERDICT: ONE REQUIRED REFINEMENT; OTHERWISE NONE FOUND.**

I accept the D2 consequence, E4/P4 level distinction, manuscript interface, and reviewer posture.

I would revise one phrase in §4:

> "mean-field theories are tested by recovery under averaging"

is slightly too categorical for a registered working interpretation. It sounds like a universal methodological rule and could overstate what the present experiment establishes.

I suggest:

> **Under MFP, the stream-level realization is tested against the MFA by recovery of the relevant projection-level predictions under the registered averaging/parity bridge.**

That makes the claim program-specific, identifies what is actually being tested, and prevents "recovery under averaging" from becoming an undefined escape hatch.

I would also make explicit one implication already latent in the registration:

**MFA parity cannot mean exact microscopic equivalence.** The parity bridge needs predeclared projection-level targets. Otherwise every mismatch can be dismissed as a finite-size/spatial departure and every rough resemblance called recovery. The bridge therefore needs the same pre-seed outcome-hygiene treatment as any other evaluative instrument.

The §5 fences are sound. In particular, the sentence barring MFP from being cited as a D2 ruling is important. My changed recommendation above is **my D2 input in light of MFP**, not a consequence logically contained in MFP itself.
