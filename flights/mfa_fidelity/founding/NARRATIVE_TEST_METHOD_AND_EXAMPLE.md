# The Narrative Test — Method and Worked Example
**Status: succession-record artifact, preserving the standing method named in Purpose and Orientation (canonical) and the worked example it cites. The method is binding on contract design; the example is the calibration standard.**

---

## 1. The method

Before any flight's contract is drafted, the flight is told **from the street**: the real-world phenomenon it examines, in the words of the people who live it, and how each experiment maps back to a question someone outside the program has actually asked. The telling must do four things:

1. **Ground truth first.** Describe the phenomenon as practitioners encounter it, before any formalism appears — the machinery earns its entrance by the phenomenon's demand, never the reverse.
2. **Map every experiment to a street question.** Each rung of the ladder must answer to something a mayor, a funder, a founder, or a delegation would recognize as their question. An experiment that maps to no street question is flagged: it may be legitimate instrument-calibration, but it must be *named* as such, not smuggled in as science.
3. **Name the strong mappings and the thin ones.** Where the apparatus abstracts (a pure common signal standing for lumpy real subsidies; a missing institutional layer), the thinness is stated in the telling so the contract cannot overclaim what the flight speaks to.
4. **End with the alignment verdict.** Does the effort, as designed, serve the goals — and is this the moment to say so, while saying so is cheap?

The test exists because documentation discipline, left ungoverned, drifts toward serving itself: the program has burned real energy on integrity machinery whose goals clouded into the machinery's own maintenance. The narrative test is the instrument that catches that drift — it re-anchors every contract to the delegations' question before a threshold is frozen. **Phenomenology outranks formalism; the telling is how the ranking is enforced.**

## 2. The worked example — the MFA-fidelity flight, told from the street

*(Preserved as delivered and accepted in the design conversation; the calibration standard for future tellings.)*

**The phenomenon.** A mid-sized city spends real money to become a startup town. Announcements are made, a fund is raised, an accelerator opens, and for two or three years the place is genuinely busy — more foundings, more events, more press. Then the program cycle ends, and one of two things happens. In most places, the activity subsides with the funding: the busy years turn out to have been *rented*. In a few places, something different occurs — the activity keeps going, and more than that, it starts to hold together: the same people keep showing up in each other's ventures, a founder's failure feeds a neighbor's launch, the town develops a self-propelling quality that no single program is paying for. Practitioners can tell these two outcomes apart on sight, and the delegations that visited Chattanooga were living the first outcome while looking at the second. What the field cannot currently say is *what the difference is, when it happens, and why money alone never buys it.* The theory's answer, in one sentence: activity and coherence are two different quantities, arriving in two different transitions, and a region can genuinely have the first without the second — that busy-but-not-an-ecosystem condition is a real, stable state of a region, not a failure of effort or a waypoint that resolves on its own.

**The hypothesis, in the words a mayor would use.** Three claims stack. First, conditions matter before anything else: whether trying can succeed here, what trying costs, whether the surroundings support it — and when those conditions clear a threshold, sustained activity appears. Second — the exposed claim — there is a *second* threshold: activity has to reach a certain thickness before what people do starts locking together, and below that thickness a town can be lively for years without ever becoming a system. Third, the two kinds of history: the activity itself is rentable and reversible, but coherent episodes soak into the ground — they change what the place *is*, so that a town that once cohered re-ignites more easily than one that was merely busy. Corollary: **busy years and coherent years leave different depths of stain** — two towns with identical activity charts can face different futures.

**What the flight builds, and why it's honest to test this way.** The small featureless city — tiles, lamps, three hidden dials, the weathering — because the real question is structural, not circumstantial. Every real region's story is entangled with its particulars; the model city strips the particulars so that if the three-regime structure appears, it appeared from the mechanism alone, and if it fails to appear, no particular can be blamed. The city has both kinds of push the real world uses: the *house light* — a uniform lift touching every lot at once, which is what a subsidy, a campaign, a region-wide story does — and the *neighbor coupling* — light near you making your own lighting likelier, which is what collision, proximity, and local relationships do. And the ground listens to activity as well as to coordination: busy-but-loose years stain the dials shallowly, locked-together years deeply, as the paper predicts.

**Each experiment, mapped to the street.**
- **E1** is the mayor's first question: *do conditions really gate activity?* Turn the ground quality up from hopeless to favorable; watch whether sustained activity switches on at a threshold rather than drifting up smoothly from nothing.
- **E2** is the flight's heart and the delegations' question run as an experiment: *can a town be genuinely busy without becoming a system — and if so, what kind of push produces that state?* Search the entire space of pushes — strong subsidy with weak neighborhoods, strong neighborhoods with no subsidy, every mix — for a stable busy-but-loose condition. The proposal under test makes a specific prediction about which pushes produce it: the busy-but-loose state should appear when activity is driven by the uniform lift (the house light) with neighbor coupling weak, and should not appear when activity is driven by the neighbor coupling itself. If the state appears only under uniform lift, the central prediction and the proposal are affirmed together, and the earlier finding — neighbor-driven activity always arrived already-organized — is explained rather than contradicted. If the state appears under no combination of pushes, the paper's central prediction fails in our worlds, and we say so.
- **E3** asks whether coherence cares about *how much* activity rather than *how the activity was produced*: reach the same busyness by different routes; see whether locking-together arrives at the same thickness regardless.
- **E4** is the stain test and the most practitioner-legible experiment: one city through busy-but-loose years, a twin through locked-together years, matched in length; end both; ask each how easily it re-ignites. Different re-ignition is the memory claim made visible — the laboratory twin of the field program's question about whether coherence outlasts its scaffolding.
- **E5**, deliberately last, asks about the *shape* of the transitions — gradual and retraceable, or sudden and sticky — the working guess held honestly as a guess, sequenced last so no answer about shape can contaminate the answers about structure.

**Where the mapping is strong and where it's thin.** Strong: E2 and E4 are the program's purpose incarnate — the delegations' question and the rebuild question — and a clean answer to either, in either direction, is worth the flight by itself. The two-channel machinery maps onto real instruments with unusual fidelity: uniform lift is what money buys; neighbor coupling is what Feld's little things build. Thin, stated so the contract does not overclaim: the city's "subsidy" is a pure common signal while real subsidies are lumpy and targeted — the flight tests the *channel*, not any policy; the coordination-infrastructure question (the ESO layer) is deliberately absent and waits its turn, so nothing here speaks to whether convening must be permanently paid for; and every finding is about simplified worlds and instruments — the flight can affirm or refute the *mechanism class*; the bridge to any actual city remains the field program's job.

**The alignment verdict, as accepted:** within those fences, every experiment answers to a question someone in a conference room actually asked — and the deepest question, *is busy-without-becoming a real place a region can be, and does it leave a shallower mark*, is exactly what the contract puts at risk.

## 3. Application rule for successors

Every future contract receives this telling **before drafting**, in this order: phenomenon → hypothesis in street words → what the apparatus builds and why it's honest → experiment-to-question map → strong and thin mappings → alignment verdict. The telling is reviewed by Mike before the contract is drafted; the contract's claims section must be consistent with the telling's thin-mapping admissions. A contract whose telling cannot be written — whose experiments answer no street question and whose abstractions cannot be honestly named — is not ready to draft. When in doubt about whether a telling passes, compare it against §2: that is what passing looks like.

*End of artifact.*
