## 1. Why this program exists

During Mike Bradshaw's years as Executive Director of CO.LAB in Chattanooga (through the reorganization in which the existing 501(c)(3), Create Here, became CO.LAB), delegations of economic-development officials visited from regions that had spent seriously — funds, facilities, programs — to build entrepreneurial ecosystems, and had gotten activity without an ecosystem. Their question, in substance: *we bought everything; why isn't it alive?* The honest answer available at the time was a heuristic (Feld's little things). This program exists to replace that heuristic with a mechanism: a formal, testable account of what separates a busy region from a coherent one, why money alone never buys the difference, and what — if anything — does.

The program's human origin is a collaboration, and the order matters: Mike and Dr. Phil Roundy met in 2014 and bonded quickly — Phil's ecosystem scholarship and Mike's practitioner experience recognizing each other — and it was that bond that sent Mike back into Prigogine, whom he had been reading for twenty years, in a deliberate effort to master science and mathematics beyond his technical training. Their 2018 paper treating ecosystems as complex adaptive systems is that effort's first published fruit, and the present program is the same twelve-year thread, unbroken. A standing motivation of the program, stated plainly because it shapes priorities: the collaboration, and the manuscript Phil is writing, are not to be put aside.

**The value being created now, by the next flight, is the foundation of everything:** does Ψ exist, and is there a Regime II? Is coherence a real, distinct quantity — and is activated-but-incoherent a real, stable condition a region can occupy? No conclusion is ordained. Affirmation and refutation are both worth the full cost of the program, because either ends the field's guessing.

**The destination, beyond this flight and beyond the MFA paper,** is a phenomenology-testing instrument: a toy city into which real-world occurrences — the subsidy landing, the accelerator opening, the champion leaving, the story catching, the funding cycle ending — can be expressed as experiments, so that hypotheses about what stimulates, maintains, or recovers an ecosystem are put at risk in silico before any region spends its money and years on them. The ESO layer (coordination infrastructure as a maintained, fundable, removable feature) and the affordance layers (environmental features that afford action differentially by configuration — spaces, events, programs, capital structures that real regions actually build and remove) are the reason for the enterprise. They are not extensions of the program; they are its purpose, waiting on its foundation.

**Why this next flight bears that future as no later flight will:** successor flights add dials; this flight fixes the chassis the dials mount on. Its regime map, channel decomposition, parity architecture, Q semantics, and observable roles become the vocabulary every future phenomenology experiment is written in. Later flights can be wrong and corrected; this flight's structural choices become the language corrections are written in.

## 2. The paramount design principle

**Phenomenology outranks formalism.** The things that actually happen to bring ecosystems about — variable, local, historical — are what the substrate exists to test. Whenever a design choice trades phenomenological capability for formal elegance, the trade is flagged and arbitrated with the same force as an ontology violation. The narrative test — telling the flight from the street before writing it in the contract — is a standing method, applied to every contract, with this program's own design conversation as the worked example of passing it.

**Variability is a testable opinion, not an assumption.** Mike's personal belief: the phenomenology of emergence is highly variable across places and times. This is held as opinion, explicitly. The design consequence is binding: the instrument must be capable of testing the variability claim and must not embed either answer. A substrate that could produce only one canonical emergence story would beg the question this program most wants asked.

**Jurisdiction travels with every result.** Findings are about simplified worlds and instruments until the field program says otherwise. The in-silico phenomenology program screens and prioritizes; it never predicts a region. The field, when it finally speaks, outranks the model.

## 3. The three claims — registered at full intent

These claims, articulated by Mike, are canonical in the following form. The technical registration (Base-Space Claims Registration v1.1) implements them; this section governs their meaning.

**Claim A (COMMITTED DERIVATION).** *"Similar base spaces means agents may act similarly under similar conditions."* The word **may** is load-bearing and original to Mike. The architecture derives: identical configurations under identical conditions have identical action propensities; nearby configurations have similar propensities only to the extent the response mapping is regular — and near critical transitions, this theory's own subject matter, regularity is precisely what cannot be assumed; and identical propensities can still realize differently. The correction cycle that produced this form converged back to Mike's original hedge; the record now says what he first said.

**Claim B (UNDER TEST, attached to the two-channel proposal).** *"Base-space similarities affect responsiveness to narrative."* In apparatus language this is configuration-gated response to the shared signal (the local-conduciveness-gated drive refinement). The abstraction to "shared signal" is deliberate and channel-faithful, **and the narrative interpretation is part of the claim's intent**: the same story lands as actionable lift where conditions can carry it and as noise where they cannot. No successor reads Claim B as drive mechanics only.

**Claim C (EXPLORATORY; two readings, both preserved).** *"Base-space similarities affect responsiveness ... to actions taken with regard to affordances in the environment."* Reading one (registered): similar-base loci respond alike to changes in the affordance environment — a single environmental shift writes correlated conduciveness moves across a base-space neighborhood, wherever its members sit geographically. Reading two (flagged, connected to the legibility refinement): similar-base loci respond alike to *other agents' observable actions engaging affordances* — a claim about coupling, about whose action counts for whom. Both readings are within Mike's intent; neither collapses into the other; the affordance layers of the future program are the eventual home of both. Claim C is the theory seed of the affordance program: differential response by configuration is what makes an affordance experiment informative at all.

**The fence that protects all three:** the claims propose similarity of *response*, never similarity *coupling*. Common-cause correlation among similarly responsive loci is permitted — and under the relevant common perturbation, predicted — without any direct similarity interaction. Similarity of conditional response is not an interaction edge. The homophily fence is the claims' guardian, not their constraint.
