## Claim A — Common base-response mapping (COMMITTED DERIVATION, as corrected by L2)

Under the committed action architecture, loci presented with **identical** base configurations and identical other action-relevant local conditions have **identical conditional action propensities** — this follows because action propensity is a common function of those inputs and no locus-specific hidden state enters it. **Nearby** configurations produce correspondingly nearby propensities **only to the extent guaranteed by the regularity of the committed response mapping** — near inputs do not entail near outputs merely by entering the same function (threshold responses are the standing counterexample, and critical-transition behavior is this theory's own subject matter). Stochastic realizations may differ even where propensities coincide; similarity lives in conditional action statistics, not individual trajectories.

*Withdrawn as overstated:* the unrestricted proposition "similar configurations behave similarly." The architecture derives **common conditional response to common inputs**; similarity claims additionally require a metric and a regularity statement, neither yet committed.

## Claim B — Configuration-gated responsiveness to the shared signal (UNDER TEST, attached to the two-channel proposal)

The shared signal may be common while the response is configuration-dependent: the same region-wide signal enters as actionable lift where local conditions can carry it and as noise where they cannot. Formal axis: additive drive (committed current form) versus **local-conduciveness-gated** drive (signal scaled by a function of the locus's own conduciveness). **Naming per L2's caution:** the gate reads the *local* substrate quantity, never the aggregate theorist-level Λ; "Λ-gated" is not used, preserving the V1 local/aggregate distinction. Fully within the ontology: responsiveness is a property of conditions.

**Substrate facts (R4, L3-confirmed from source):** a gating implementation modifies only the drive arithmetic (Step 4 of the Lineage A core) plus one telemetry column for the gating factor; it adds no PRNG draws and does not reorder existing ones, so it preserves the conditional bit-exact Lineage A parity gate.

## Claim C — Base-space-indexed common influence (EXPLORATORY; arbitration required before contract entry)

Loci similar in base space are afforded-to by the same environmental features; a single environmental change writes correlated conduciveness shifts across a base-space neighborhood wherever its members sit geographically. This is a genuinely different sharing geometry — geographic adjacency, region-wide broadcast, and base-space-defined common exposure are not parameter values of one channel. Held outside contracts pending Mike's arbitration.

## The registered distinction — common-cause correlation without coupling (as corrected by L2)

**Common-cause correlation among similarly responsive loci is permitted — and, under the relevant common perturbation, predicted — without any direct similarity coupling.** Correlation does not arise from similarity alone; there must be shared variation to which the loci's conditional responses covary. Discriminator: correlation-without-coupling predicts co-movement only when a common cause moves; true similarity-coupling would correlate similar loci in quiet environments as well. The homophily fence stands in L2's formulation: **similarity of conditional response is not an interaction edge.** Similarity-gated interaction remains excluded absent Mike's arbitration as a new mechanism class.

## D6's protective scope (narrowed per L2)

**D6 (i.i.d. initialization) suppresses spatial signatures arising from organized base-space arrangement; it does not suppress configuration-dependent response heterogeneity itself.** Heterogeneity correlated with base configuration may be visible in the current flight and is not a D6 violation. Registered consequence for the successor arrangement experiment: once bases become spatially correlated, geographic structure can arise through two separable axes — base arrangement × ordinary response mapping, and base arrangement × gated common-signal response — and the arrangement contract must carry both as separable design axes to attribute its own effects.
