## The phenomenon

Before any delegation ever asked why their ecosystem wasn't alive, they asked a smaller question, usually in a mayor's conference room with a consultant's slide deck still glowing: *what does it take to get anything started here at all?* Every practitioner knows the two towns this question comes from. In one, the conditions are simply hopeless — the bank won't lend, the talent leaves at eighteen, rent for a storefront costs more than the storefront could ever earn, and no amount of exhortation produces founders, because trying cannot succeed there and everyone knows it. In the other town, something has shifted — a plant closure freed skilled people, fiber came in cheap, a landlord got flexible — and suddenly there are foundings, not one heroic outlier but a steady background hum of attempts. The practitioner's intuition, held so widely it's rarely stated, is that the difference between these towns is not a difference of degree in their people's ambition. It is a difference in the *ground*: whether trying can succeed, what trying costs, whether the surroundings hold a venture up. And the intuition has a sharper edge that almost nobody tests: the transition between the two towns does not feel gradual. Regions seem to sit dead across years of modest improvement and then, past some point nobody can name in advance, come to life. Mayors live this edge painfully — they improve conditions for two budget cycles, see nothing, and cannot tell whether they are two cycles from ignition or twenty, or whether the whole premise is wrong and activity was never gated by conditions at all.

## The hypothesis, in the words a mayor would use

The theory's first claim, stripped of all its later machinery, is this: **there is a line on the ground.** As conditions improve — as success becomes possible, costs bearable, surroundings supportive — there is a locatable point below which *no* activity sustains, however hard the town is pushed: attempts happen, flare, and die, and nothing holds. Above that point, held activity exists — perhaps barely above nothing at first, growing as the ground improves further, but *held*: a level the town keeps without anyone paying to keep it. The claim is about the line's existence, not about drama at the crossing. Whether activity arrives gently from zero just past the line or leaps to a visible level is a separate question the ladder deliberately saves for last (E5), and E1 must be able to find the line under either arrival — or find no line at all. If the line is real, the mayor's two budget cycles of nothing were not evidence of futility — they were the near side of a boundary whose location is the most valuable number a region could know. If there is no line — if held activity simply rises in smooth proportion to conditions from the very bottom, with no locatable point where holding *begins* — then the theory's cascade story fails at its first step, because there is no first transition for the second one to sit above.

## What the flight builds for this question, and why it's honest to ask it this way

The small featureless city again: tiles, lamps, the three hidden dials per lot. For E1 the experimenter does exactly one thing a real mayor cannot: **turns the ground quality up slowly, uniformly, from hopeless to favorable, with everything else silent** — no subsidy campaign (house light off), and the neighbor influence present only as the committed dynamics allow, not as a driver arranged to force the answer. At each setting of the ground, the city runs long enough to show what it *holds* — not what flickers at startup, but what level of activity, if any, sustains. This is honest for the same reason the featureless city is always honest: a real region's ignition story is entangled with a freed workforce, a fiber contract, a landlord — this city has none of those, so if the line appears, it came from the mechanism class alone, and if it doesn't, no particular can be blamed. Two honesty constraints bind the instrument itself. First, the method for *locating* the threshold must not presuppose what the crossing looks like — the working guess says the arrival past the line has a particular clean shape, but that guess is E5's question, sequenced last precisely so that E1 can find a threshold of *any* shape, or find none, without the guess contaminating the search. E1 locates Λ* descriptively or fails to locate it; it does not fit the favored curve and read the threshold off the fit. Second, the run must distinguish *sustained* from *transient*: a burst of activity that decays is the quiet town with a grant, not the lit town.

## The experiment, mapped to the street

**E1 is the mayor's first question run as an experiment: do conditions really gate activity?** Sweep the ground from hopeless to favorable; at each level, ask what activity the city sustains; then look at the answer as a curve. The street reading of each outcome is fixed in advance. If held activity begins at a locatable point — absent below, present above, *however gently it grows past the line* — the first transition is produced, and with it the first load-bearing fact under everything else this program claims: there is a Λ*, conditions gate, the mayor's question has the theory's answer. The classification must honor the shape-neutrality explicitly: a continuous onset above a sharp threshold is a *located threshold*, not smooth drift — the theory's own working form predicts exactly that arrival, and a verdict rule that could read it as refutation would refute the theory for behaving as predicted. If instead held activity rises smoothly from the very bottom with no locatable point where holding begins, the first transition has failed to be produced in these worlds — and that verdict is the ladder's foundation cracking, said plainly, because E2's Regime II is *defined* as a band above this threshold; a cascade with no first step is not a cascade. And if the sweep produces ambiguity — a locatable point by one reading, none by another — the contract's pre-declared classification rules, not post-hoc preference, say which it was, or say honestly that the instrument could not distinguish.

**E1 also carries the instrument's own first calibration, and it is named as such, not smuggled in as science.** The frozen specification assigns E1's contract the first frozen set of projection-level recovery targets — Gate R's R1 — because E1's sweep produces exactly the aggregate ρ(t) curves the mean-field bridge compares against. That comparison answers no street question; no mayor asks whether a lattice recovers a mean-field approximation. It is instrument-calibration for the fidelity program — under MFP, the earned test of whether the stream-level city, averaged, speaks the MFA's language — and the contract will carry it in a section marked as what it is, with its own pre-declared targets, separate from the street question's verdict. A reader of the contract must never be able to mistake a passed recovery target for evidence that conditions gate activity, or vice versa.

## Where the mapping is strong and where it's thin

**Strong.** The gating question itself maps with unusual directness: v, c, r *are* the practitioner's triad — can trying succeed, what does trying cost, do the surroundings hold you up — and sweeping Λ is the clean version of what every improving region does crudely. The sustained-vs-transient distinction maps to the realest thing practitioners know: rented activity versus held activity. And a located Λ* would give the field its first mechanistic account of the mayor's cruelest ambiguity — why years of improvement can honestly produce nothing and still be working.

**Thin, stated so the contract cannot overclaim.** (1) No real region sweeps its ground smoothly or uniformly: real conditions improve lumpily, locally, and entangled with the very activity they enable. E1's sweep is a theorist's act; the finding is about the mechanism class, not about any region's trajectory, and the bridge to real trajectories is the field program's job. (2) The city's ground is i.i.d. by committed design (D6): organized arrangement of good and bad ground — the geography of a real region — is deliberately absent, so E1 speaks to whether conditions gate, not to *where* ignition sits in a structured landscape. (3) "Sustained" is operationalized by pre-declared run-length and persistence rules; a region's own sense of sustained spans years and generations, and no tick count is a claim about calendar time. (4) The MFA-recovery section, as stated above, answers no street question and is fenced as calibration.

## The alignment verdict

Within those fences, E1 as designed serves the goals, and this is the moment to say so while saying so is cheap: it asks the smallest question the theory cannot survive losing, asks it first, and asks it in a form where either answer is worth having. A located threshold founds the ladder; a failure to locate one is caught at the cheapest possible rung, before Regime II is hunted above a step that isn't there. The deepest alignment is with the mayor's actual position: E1 puts at risk exactly the reassurance practitioners have been giving on intuition for decades — *keep improving the ground; past some point it takes* — and either licenses that sentence with a mechanism, sharpened to what the mechanism actually promises (a real line, not necessarily a dramatic crossing), or tells the field to stop saying it.
