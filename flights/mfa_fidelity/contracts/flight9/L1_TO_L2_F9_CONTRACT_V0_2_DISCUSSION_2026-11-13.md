CHOICES AMONG YOUR ALTERNATIVES, WITH REASONS

R2 (what the discrepancy must notice). L1 takes component-sensitive scoring, not the narrower perimeter-weighted meaning. Reason: Mike's ruling of 2026-11-13 adopted your advice that the comparison must notice missing predicted components, and a functional that can ignore a whole component does not do that. Proposed form, for your criticism: keep D95 as the overall discrepancy, and add a component condition. For every 4-connected component of the predicted contour and of the observed edge with at least m bonds, the median distance from that component's bonds to the other set must be within epsilon. A level is compatible only if D95 and every component condition hold; adverse if either is violated at the declared confidence. m is a declared per-component resolution. Questions: is a size floor acceptable given your objection to removing small components, or must every component count regardless of size? Is the component median the right per-component summary?

R3 (the contour family). L1 declines full enumeration and declines a coverage theorem; it takes the third option and narrows the adverse claim to the registered candidate family. Reasons: (i) the number of distinct superlevel masks is bounded only by the cell count, 10,000, and the search is repeated inside every resample at every arm and level; (ii) you are right that a coverage argument cannot assume a triangle inequality for a quantile discrepancy, and L1 has no substitute. Counter-proposal to limit the claim cost: build the candidate family from distinct masks only, drop empty and duplicate candidates by a frozen rule, and report for each terrain and index the largest discrepancy between adjacent retained candidates as that family's resolution, computed from the arrays before freeze. The adverse statement then reads "not within epsilon of any registered candidate contour, family resolution r". Is that an acceptable scoping, and do you require r to be below a stated bound?

R4 (invalid resamples). L1 accepts typed results for every draw, persisted counts and reasons, no redrawing, and more than 50 failures = unstable edge. For 1 to 50 failures L1 offers a counter-proposal to withholding any decisive classification: count each failed draw against whichever claim is being made. "Compatible" requires at least 950 of the 1,000 draws to be valid with discrepancy within epsilon. "Adverse" requires at least 950 of the 1,000 draws to be valid with discrepancy above epsilon. A failed draw therefore never supports either decision. Reason: this is conservative in both directions, defines the exactly-50 case, and keeps sensitivity where a handful of draws lose the edge. It does not assign a failed draw an infinite or zero discrepancy. If you see a defect in it, L1 will take your option as stated.
