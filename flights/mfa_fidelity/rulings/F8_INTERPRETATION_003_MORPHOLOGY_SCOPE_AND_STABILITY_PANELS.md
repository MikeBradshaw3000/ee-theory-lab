## Part 1 — the morphology report (H1, route a)

**The conflict.** §7.3 item 3 has the realization census record "the M4 morphology label against §1.3(a)", and §4.4/§4.7 define that label on the band after refinement — the final level set, pass-1 levels plus kept insertions. Placed M1 (`f8/config.py::f8_run_spec`, `43ea8de23c0d718b…`, L2 M1 r1 D1) accepts production-shape runs on the pass-1 grid only; an insertion level cannot be run at production shape without changing a ratified module. §4.7(2) and §4.9 make the M4 verdict bear on nothing in P1 and pre-register its expected result (rule 2, `no null-consistent phase`, which requests no insertions).

**The interpretation (binding).**

1. **The morphology record of this stage is a restricted secondary report on the pass-1 level set.** M7's `MorphologyRecord` computes M4's verdict and router decision from the realization census's 24 pass-1 labels and records every insertion the router requests as an `EvaluabilityHalt` with its level; `verdict_scope` is the literal string `pass-1 level set`. The record never writes `final level set`.
2. **Completion and pass status.** The record is **complete** when all 24 pass-1 labels are present and the halts equal the router's requested insertions exactly; it is accepted only if it replays from the authorized level records. Two fields state what the pass-1 verdict covers: `final_set_identical_to_pass1` is `True` iff the router requests no insertion — then the pass-1 verdict IS the final-level-set verdict — and `final_set_obligation` is `"discharged_identically"` in that case and `"open"` otherwise. When insertions are requested, the §1.3(a) comparison (the record's `matches_expectation`) is descriptive, of pass-1 scope, and is not a discharge of the §4.4 final-set obligation, which the package records as open. Package predicates: `morphology_pass1_evaluated` (complete, scope `pass-1 level set`) and `morphology_final_set_identical` (the obligation discharged identically).
3. **No effect on P1.** §4.7(2) stands: nothing in this record enters the §4.11 evaluation. The restriction changes what the secondary report can say, not what P1 is.
4. **What would close the obligation.** A successor instrument version with a run grammar for insertion levels at production shape (a disclosed M1 change, reviewed by L2, with its own run count) — not an edit to M7 and not a reading of this record.
5. **Scope.** This interpretation governs F8 M7's morphology record only. It does not reopen §6 (routing slack), Appendix A, or M4.

## Part 2 — the stability seed panels (H2, route a)

**The conflict.** §7.3 item 2 reads "`skeleton_stability` — alternative templates and seed panels; class reproduced within Δm"; item 4 carries `realization_stability` under E1-R's grammar. M5-3 (ratification record §B) resolved "alternative templates" as grid-resolution and solver independence for the closure; it said nothing about seed panels. §3.4 and §7.3 item 0 make the calibration panel "never scored, never reused". M7 round 1 scored it.

**The interpretation (binding).**

6. **One independent stability seed panel, `F8_STABILITY_SEED_PANEL`, of twenty seeds, declared in M1.** Placed M1 (`f8/config.py`, `43ea8de23c0d718b…`, L2 M1 r1 D1) declares exactly two seed panels and requires every production-shape seed to come from one of them, so a third panel is not executable without a change to M1's frozen declaration. **Mike's ruling (2026-11-02, "a′"): a disclosed M1 touch, `config.py` and its test module only**, placed with M7 and reviewed by L2 in the same round. The touch adds, by M1's own convention for its panels (prefix + build date, drawn once at build, hardcoded with a digest literal): `F8_STABILITY_MASTER = 920261102` (prefix 9, build date 2026-11-02), `F8_STABILITY_SEED_PANEL = generate_seed_panel(F8_STABILITY_MASTER)`, `F8_STABILITY_PANEL_SHA256_LITERAL`, the panel `stability` in `f8_run_spec` (production-shape seeds from that panel only; objects `realization` and `skeleton_high` only, mirroring the calibration rule), and the two fields in M1's declaration, whose digest is re-established (`0d9657d79c8b1c63…` → the touched module's). The panel is disjoint from the canonical and calibration panels and from E1's; M1's preflight refuses otherwise. Only M7 binds M1's declaration digest (M2–M6 and M-P1 do not), so the touch cascades nowhere else. No stability run exists before this declaration, so the master is drawn outcome-free. 
7. **What the stability jobs run.** `skeleton_stability`: `skeleton_high` over the 24 pass-1 levels × the stability panel (480 runs). `realization_stability`: `realization` over the same levels × the same panel (480 runs). Both at production shape, under their own job authorization and the §7.1 readiness gate, after the canonical census of the same object is committed. Level records carry `panel = "stability"`. The assembled sweep is persisted as M7's own record `StabilitySweep` (object, panel, stability master, the level-record identities, and inside it an M-P1 `SweepRecord` carrying M-P1's non-canonical role `synthetic` — M-P1's role alphabet is `canonical | synthetic` and no placed module changes under this record); it is a canonical OUTPUT of its job, is never a `canonical` sweep, and never enters P1.
8. **What "class reproduced" means (declared, M7-5 as revised).** Skeleton: T-S1′ on the stability sweep yields the same crossing-interval index as the canonical skeleton sweep, or both yield the same adverse cause. Realization: T-R1′ on the stability sweep (against the calibration census bands) yields the same outcome and cause as the canonical realization sweep. Both are instrument-reproducibility statements; neither enters P1; a failed reproduction is recorded, not adjudicated, in this stage.
9. **The calibration panel's data role is unchanged:** never scored, never reused — M7 may read job-0 level records only to replay the canonical census (§7.3 item 0) and to build the tolerance ensembles and program (M5).
10. **Resource count.** The three lattice jobs' 2,400 production-shape runs become **3,360** with the two stability jobs (960 more). `PRODUCTION_RUNS` is corrected; the benchmark's projection follows. At 1.3 s/run this is ≈ 21 min per 960-run job on the canonical machine.
