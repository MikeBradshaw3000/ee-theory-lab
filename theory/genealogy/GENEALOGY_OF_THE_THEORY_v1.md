# Genealogy of the Theory of Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Emergence

**Status.** Genealogy tier. Version 1, 2026-10-11. A record of where the theory came from, kept so that it can be recalled when needed. It governs nothing: no claim here is architecture, no date here binds a flight, and where this document and the Overview differ on what the theory *says*, the Overview governs. Set down by Claude from Mike's statements of record in the off-protocol theory-development conversation of 2026-10-10/11, from the lake-vision note of the same date, and from the committed repository; sources are named where they exist. Brackets mark items awaiting Mike's word.

**How to use it.** Read it when a question arises about why the theory has the shape it has, which parts are old and which are recent, what a superseded formulation was and why it was superseded, or how to characterize the work's origins in a manuscript. The epistemic stratification in §9 is the part most likely to be needed.

## 1. The roots (1991 onward)

Mike's study of Ilya Prigogine and David Bohm dates from 1991, during his undergraduate degree at Georgetown, taken at forty. He wrote on Bohm's ontological interpretation of quantum theory. Prigogine's homage to Bohm, in an essay collection [citation to confirm: probably *Quantum Implications: Essays in Honour of David Bohm*, Hiley & Peat, eds., 1987], has been an object of study since; Mike describes himself as having been trying to understand it for thirty-five years.

What these two sources supply is not a formalism but the two convictions the theory never gave up. From Bohm: that a whole can be real without being a thing any part possesses — the ecosystem as a collective mode. From Prigogine: that order of this kind is a matter of *becoming*, sustained only while driven, and that irreversibility is physical rather than apparent. The seminal core of the theory — *coherence in entrepreneurial ecosystems is real, emergent, and structurally determined* — is these two convictions applied to a phenomenon Mike had watched for years as a practitioner. The seminal core predates every equation in the theory and would survive the failure of any of them.

## 2. The lake (2016–2018)

During the work that became the 2018 paper, Mike carried a mental image he calls the lake vision. It was an image of wave interference: disturbances on a surface spreading, overlapping, and producing lines of constructive interference that belonged to no single disturbance. He learned what he could about wave interference and tried to express the image, and could not.

Three facts about the image are of record:

- It was not a geographic picture exclusively. It was a metaphor for *action in an EE space that was not yet defined*. The two-space distinction (geographic space versus the space of structural conditions) came years later, and the lake image predates it; the image should not be asked to carry that distinction. (This corrects an earlier guard in the Annex, which stated that the lake surface is geographic space.)
- The mapping was already present, even unexpressed: lattice as terrain; periodic action as drops; **coherence as constructive interference**. The theory's second-order variable, Ψ, is called *coherence* because of the lake. The name is interference vocabulary, and it is the oldest named element of the formalism.
- Its limitation, statable only in hindsight, is exact: the image is linear superposition. The sources disturb the surface at their own rate regardless of the surface above them; the pattern is present in exact proportion to the forcing and dies when the forcing stops. The pattern is coherent but not emergent in the sense of a transition: nothing qualitatively new comes into being at a boundary, because nothing acts back.

[Mike has given both 2016 and "probably 2018" for the construct's origin; recorded here as 2016–2018, during the work on the 2018 paper, pending one date.]

## 3. The 2018 paper and the dangling question

Roundy, P. T., Bradshaw, M., & Brockman, B. K. (2018). The emergence of entrepreneurial ecosystems: A complex adaptive systems approach. *Journal of Business Research*, 86, 1–10.

The paper made the right claim at the depth then available: entrepreneurial ecosystems are collective order arising from many local interactions, with the properties — emergence, nonlinearity, path dependence, adaptation — of complex adaptive systems. It named properties. It did not supply a mechanism, and it could not derive a transition. Mike had, in his words, plenty of time afterwards to see that the paper lacked substance.

The question it left dangling is the lake's limitation stated for the paper: *what makes coherence a transition rather than a reflection of its inputs — what acts back?* The current theory's first transition is the answer (§7). The working relationship that produced the paper — Mike declares the claim and sets down the reasoning; Phil writes — is the one producing the current manuscript.

## 4. The call (2021–2022)

Two documents and one conversation.

- Roundy, P. T., & Lyons, T. S. (2023). Where are the entrepreneurs? A call to theorize the micro-foundations and strategic organization of entrepreneurial ecosystems. *Strategic Organization*, 21(2), 447–459; first published online 18 October 2021. Coleman (1990) is in its reference list.
- Cowen, Rink, Cuypers, Grégoire, & Weller (2022). From the Editors — Applying Coleman's boat in management research: Opportunities and challenges in bridging macro and micro theory. *Academy of Management Journal*, 65(1), 1–10 (February 2022). The editors name three theoretical failures of cross-level work — misapplied micro theory (arrow 2), thin macro context (arrow 1), neglected aggregation (arrow 3) — and write that "despite the prevalence of microfoundations research, aggregation is rarely studied theoretically," that reducibility "requires a clear aggregation logic that models and shows how micro-level behaviors and interactions produce the macro-level outcome," and that, because of equifinality, "from the (macro-level) outcome alone, researchers can seldom identify the specific aggregation mechanism that generated the collective result."
- **March 2022.** Tom Lyons and Phil Roundy came to Mike's office with their essay and the editorial in hand and introduced him to Coleman's boat — the macro–micro–macro diagram of *Foundations of Social Theory* (1990), with its arrows named by Hedström and Ylikoski as situational, action-formation, and transformational mechanisms. Mike studied it and took it as a call to action. He began thinking and writing again.

Corrections of record: the call was in AMJ, not AMR, as first recalled; the visit was March 2022, not early 2023.

What Coleman supplied was a shape the theory would eventually fill completely. In the current theory: arrow 1 is the terrain (each location maps to a distribution of situational conditions, and Λ as locally configured is the only channel from conditions to a stream); arrow 2 is orientation realized as projection, with no choice-theoretic content; arrow 3 is the compression of action streams into ρ and Ψ and the two transitions; and a fourth arrow Coleman's single pass lacks, macro outcome to macro conditions over time, is Q. Coleman called the micro-to-macro transition the central unsolved problem; the normal-form apparatus (§6) is a theory of that arrow and nothing else.

A later item belongs here for completeness: the *Journal of Business Venturing* special issue "The Microfoundations of Entrepreneurial Ecosystems" (guest editors Audretsch, Cavallo, Colombo, Roundy, Stam; call posted December 2024) cites Coleman (1990) and names "arrows 1 and 3" as the unresolved links. The question the theory answers is being asked, by the people whose answer it is.

## 5. The long approach (2022–2025)

Mike's own account, as set down in the lake-vision note: progress after 2018 was not linear. There was a long period of deepening in Prigogine, and then an attempt to bring symmetry, quantum theory, and dynamics to a micro description of macro emergence. That attempt failed — not from confusion, but because the jump from statistical mechanics to social ontology requires bridging work nobody hands you; it has to be discovered by failing at it directly. The route ran through quantum theory to quantum field theory. ChatGPT, capable by 2023, tutored Mike in mathematics and calculus through this period; Claude was encountered along the way.

A reading offered for Mike's confirmation rather than asserted: what QFT holds at its center is spontaneous symmetry breaking, and Landau's mean-field theory is its classical ancestor. If so, the detour was not a wrong turn but a route to the formalism at the wrong level, followed by the descent to the level at which it could be stated without fields in the physicist's sense. ## 6. Landau (28 March 2026)

The Landau formalization is dated: 28 March 2026. From that date the theory has a hard core of formalization distinct from its seminal core: two ordered transitions; an activation density ρ and a coherence Ψ as order parameters; the sign-change mechanism μ(ρ) governing the second transition; the feedback operator Q; the action ontology (action streams as primitive; no decision, utility, or interiority); and base independence (v, c, r composed multiplicatively into Λ, with Q acting on the bases individually). Haken's synergetics is the bridge by which Landau's apparatus is licensed for a social system: the normal-form theorem concerns variables and their symmetries, not matter.

**The hysteresis stage, superseded.** An early formulation carried two thresholds — Λc↑ for ignition and Λc↓ for collapse — with a bistable region between them, and treated the resulting "hysteresis leaf" as the theory's signature shape. It was superseded when it was found that feedback and the updating of Λ come through Q: the system's memory lives in the slow reshaping of the structural bases, not in the geometry of the bifurcation. The transitions were then written in continuous (supercritical) form, and path dependence was located in Q. Overview §15 states the result and the reason. [Date of the supersession: Mike to supply.] Any document that still shows a hysteresis leaf describes this superseded stage.

Regime II — activated but incoherent — is a postulate of the formalization, not part of the seminal core. It is the theory's central empirical prediction and the part most exposed to failure.

## 7. April 2026: the lake drawn, and the program inverted

Two things happened in April 2026.

The lake was illustrated for the first time: the Lattice Wave Interference animation, built with Claude — an N×N grid of sources each emitting a Gaussian wave packet at a fixed interval; packets spread, interfere, and decay; boundaries reflect; a Sobel-gradient toggle shows the ridges of constructive interference, the quasi-particle lines that slide across the lattice in the transients. Mike had never heard of Gaussian packets; they were exactly what the image needed, and the image became expressible eight years after it formed. (The lake-vision note of 2026-10-11 gives "around 2019" for the animation; that is in error. The animation is April 2026, and it is the first illustration.)

In the same month the modeling program inverted: theory first, agents deliberately empty of behavioral hypotheses, the lattice as a stochastic instrument for testing whether a collective-level theory survives finite realization. The first discussion of participation and of flicker — intermittent registration at the activation surface — is from the chat of 7 April 2026.

The lake-vision note records, as a possible next step, a version of the animation in which each source's drop rate responds to the local field amplitude, so that ridges persist after the forcing stops. In the program's vocabulary that is local-conduciveness-gated feedback with a persistence channel: the flicker mechanism of Overview §10 and the state-memory rule that the successor contract (Flight 8) is being built to realize. The first transition, stated at stream level, is the lake with feedback installed: a population of intermittently participating streams whose collective rate of participation stops decaying.

## 9. Epistemic stratification

Three strata, each able to lose the one above it without losing itself.

| Stratum | Content | Since | Can fail how |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seminal core** | Coherence in entrepreneurial ecosystems is real, emergent, and structurally determined; the ecosystem is a collective mode no agent possesses; the right level of description is the collective. | 1991 roots; stated for ecosystems 2016–2018 | Only by the phenomenon turning out not to exist. Not a formal claim; not refutable by any flight. |
| **Hard core of the formalization** | Two ordered transitions; ρ and Ψ as order parameters; μ(ρ) sign change; Q acting on the bases; action ontology; base independence; Λ as the sole channel from conditions to streams. | 28 March 2026 | By a demonstrated failure of the two-transition architecture itself — e.g., no second transition distinct from the first. Would send the seminal core in search of different mathematics; would not refute it. |
| **Committed working hypotheses** | The continuous form of both transitions; the transcritical form for ρ (v1.8); the pitchfork for Ψ; memory in Q rather than in bifurcation geometry; the minimal three-term activation equation. | Hysteresis stage superseded [date]; v1.8 11 October 2026 | By the computational program failing to produce the predicted structure under a pre-registered rule, or by empirical findings of jumps and hysteresis. Revised under Overview §15 with the hard core intact. |

Regime II sits at the hard-core level as a prediction, with the working hypotheses supplying its form.
