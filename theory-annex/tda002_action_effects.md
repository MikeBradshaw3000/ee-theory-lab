# Action Effects: How Acts Change Structural Conditions, and Why the Same Act Does Not Have the Same Effect Twice

**Status:** Exploratory-tier working document; Annex entry TDA-002 (charter placeholder now filled). Drafted 2026-09-06 from the September action-effects discussion. Purpose: the theory's agents act, and some actions change the structural conditions (v, c, r) that shape everyone's action streams — a mayor's speech, a new fund, a district built. This document establishes how such effects are represented without violating the architecture, why their magnitude is not a property of the act, and where the one genuine extension beyond the committed formalism is flagged. Builds directly on TDA-001 (the two-spaces decomposition; footprint and displacement are defined there and used here) and connects to TDA-003 (whose ruling D3 closed off an illegal representation this document records). Written, per Annex practice, to be readable without prior physics training.

---

## 1. The problem, stated through the standing example

A mayor gives a speech in support of the region's entrepreneurial community. Observation says three things at once: the speech affects different agents' action streams differently; the mayor's act seems to reach the whole region at once, unlike most acts; and the "size" of the effect seems impossible to pin down — the same speech that transforms one moment falls flat in another.

Each observation tempts a violation. Different effects on different agents tempts *dispositional* vocabulary (some agents are more receptive); regional reach tempts making the mayor a special kind of agent with privileged dynamics; and variable magnitude tempts treating effect-size as a property of acts, to be ranked and catalogued. The architecture handles all three without any of these moves, and the handling is this document's content.

## 2. Heterogeneous effect lives in the terrain, not in the agents

The ontology forbids agents interpreting or responding differently by disposition — there is no channel for it, since structural conditions reach agents only through Λ as locally configured. So "the speech affects different streams differently" must cash out entirely on the conditions side, and it does, twice over.

First, the act itself is a *spatially structured perturbation*: in TDA-001's terms, it has a footprint (the geographic support over which it lands) and a displacement (the shift δ(v, c, r) it writes at each supported location) — and the displacement need not be uniform across the footprint. A speech lands hard in some places and soft in others; δr varies over the map. Second, even where the perturbation is identical, the agents receiving it sit at different situational base-space points (TDA-001's refinement; TDA-003's position channel), hence at different distances |Λ − Λ*| from the activation surface. An agent already deep in the supercritical region barely registers the shift; one just below the surface may be carried across; one far below moves without observable change.

Heterogeneous response, zero heterogeneous interpretation. The variation lives in the geometry of the perturbation and the geometry of the situations — exactly where the theory says structure lives. Note what the speech's *content* has become under this description: nothing. The formalism registers a perturbation with a footprint and a displacement; rhetoric, charisma, and argument enter only insofar as they change those two objects. This is a feature, not a blind spot — it is the compression discipline (TDA-006) applied to interventions.

## 3. The three legal representations

What kind of object is the mayor's act inside the committed formalism? Three representations are legal, corresponding to three kinds of act, and choosing among them is a modeling decision about the act's structure and persistence — not about the actor.

**3.1 Fluctuation.** A transient, unstructured act — one speech, no persistence, no particular spatial organization — belongs in η(t), the noise term. It jiggles the system and decays. This is not a dismissive classification: near a transition, fluctuations are decisive — the branch coherence takes is selected by exactly such jiggles. Most acts, most of the time, are legally and adequately noise.

**3.2 Exogenous structured forcing.** An act with persistence and spatial structure — a program, a fund, a district under construction — is a time-dependent perturbation applied to the base fields from outside the agent population. Architecturally this is the same species as the driving in the genealogical lake image: the rocks pushed on the grid are exogenous forcing, and the office of the mayor, acting *as* an office, is a boundary condition of the system rather than an agent within it. Nothing privileged is granted; the representation simply locates institutional action where the formalism can hold it.

**3.3 Q-realization: transduction of the macro state.** The subtlest and most interesting case. If the speech *recounts and frames what is already happening* — "look at what is being built here" — it injects no content of its own; it carries the macro state (ρ, Ψ) back down into the bases, chiefly r. That is precisely Q's job description. On this reading the mayor is not a special agent but part of Q's *realization* — one of the concrete mechanisms by which the slow feedback loop physically operates in a real region. This is also where narrative's committed role lives: narrative operates through Q, and the transduction reading is the mechanism-level statement of that commitment. The mayor's apparent specialness then dissolves into a difference of degree: some actors' transductions have wide footprints. Reach, not kind.

The three representations are not exclusive — a real speech may be part noise, part forcing, part transduction — but each part must be one of the three. There is no fourth committed channel, and Section 6 records why the tempting fourth is flagged rather than used.

## 4. Magnitude dissolved: effect is a joint property of act and state

"How big was that action?" is malformed, and the Landau framework does not so much answer it as replace it. The effect of an act is a joint property of the act and the system's state at the moment it lands. The act contributes footprint, displacement length, and displacement *direction* (which matters because F is not isotropic — near the activation surface, a small push along one axis can cross it while a large push along another does nothing; TDA-001 §2.2). The state contributes *susceptibility*: how strongly the system responds to a given push, which grows without bound as a transition is approached. The identical speech is inert deep in Regime I, produces a modest transient in mid-Regime II, and near the μ(ρ) sign-change can be the fluctuation that selects the branch.

For the narrative and the manuscript, this is a gift rather than a difficulty, because it is the theory's explanation for two of the most stubborn regularities in EE practice. First: the same intervention playbook produces transformation in one region and nothing in another — because effect depends on where the region sits relative to the transition surfaces, not on the playbook. Second: post-hoc accounts persistently credit some particular speech, event, or meeting with outsized causal power — and the theory says the folklore is recording something real. The act was ordinary; the system was critical. Near a transition, *some* small perturbation was going to be the one that tipped it, and history remembers whichever one did. Chattanooga's own folklore contains such moments, and the theory neither debunks nor mystifies them: it gives state-dependence as their mechanism.

## 5. The D3 catch, recorded here because this is where it bites

TDA-003's decision D3 rejected the idea that partial projectors (agents registering on only some bases) constitute a structural constituency, and the decisive ground belongs in this document too: the supporting intuition — that a mentor's actions "feed Q" by modifying the landscape other agents encounter — is an architecture violation. Q reads only (ρ, Ψ). No individual's actions reach the terrain directly; micro detail is coarse-grained away before the terrain hears anything. An act can only touch the bases through the three representations of Section 3, and the first two are *modeling* representations of exogenous structure, not channels available to in-population agents. The intuition that individuals visibly reshape conditions for others is not wrong about the world; it is a claim the committed formalism deliberately does not carry — which is the subject of Section 6.

## 6. The flagged extension: direct micro-to-terrain coupling

The fourth option — a channel by which an individual agent's actions write directly to the base fields, with actor-specific footprints — would be a genuine extension of the architecture, not an interpretation of it. Two independent discussions converged on this same missing door: the mayor question (what if the speech is neither noise, nor exogenous, nor pure transduction, but an in-population agent's act with real terrain consequences?) and the partial-projector question (the mentor whose actions constitute the reinforcement others encounter). Convergence from independent directions is usually a sign the door is real.

The extension is flagged, not built, for principled reasons: it would need care not to breach base independence (an actor whose acts write to multiple bases could smuggle in cross-coupling that Q's diagonal structure exists to forbid); it would complicate the sufficient-statistic bottleneck (TDA-006 Part I) by letting micro detail bypass (ρ, Ψ); and nothing in the current empirical program requires it. It belongs to the follow-up-paper horizon. Until then, the committed position is austere and adequate: within the formalism, individual agents change the world only by acting or not — their collective registration, compressed to (ρ, Ψ), is the only voice the terrain ever hears.
